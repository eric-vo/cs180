Now, to generate new images, I trained another model on pure noise instead. Here are the results from that training:
<pure_noise_denoised_results_1.png>
<pure_noise_denoised_results_5.png>
<pure_noise_denoise_loss.png>
What patterns are visible? The generated outputs are all very similar, resembling an average of all 10 possible digits. This pattern may exist since from pure noise, we have no indication of what particular digit an image may be/become. Thus, the model turns pure noise into an average of all possible digits.

Since one-step denoising doesn't work well, let's train a flow matching model, instead. I trained a UNet conditioned on time for 10 epochs to predict the velocity of a noisy image given a particular timestep. The results of the training loss are below:

In this class-conditioned UNet, I used a learning rate decay scheduler to reduce the learning rate over time. What if we removed the scheduler? I tried just that. To compensate for the loss of the scheduler, I simply lowered the learning rate from 1e-2 to 1e-4 to ensure the model converges smoothly on a minimum during training.
<class_simple_reduced_learning_loss.png>
<flow_conditional_samples_1_simple_reduced_learning.png>
<flow_conditional_samples_5_simple_reduced_learning.png>
<flow_conditional_samples_10_simple_reduced_learning.png>
And just like that, we can generate results that are just as good as when we had the scheduler!
